Resistant Bacteria

Antibiotics Evolution: A change in the inherited characteristics of a population (a group of the same species) over time. In 1960 an antibiotic ('methicillin') was introduced for treating infections caused by a species of bacterium: Staphylococcus aureus. It was very successful at killing the bacteria at first. But by 1961, MRSA were identified. Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus. Methicillin-resistance was an inherited characteristic, so there was a change in the inherited characteristics in the population of S. aureus. The population had evolved. This evolution was a result of natural selection and rendered methicillin useless for treating the bacterium. When a patient takes methicillin after a random mutation allowed a resistance to methicillin to evolve, all the non-resistant bacteria were killed. Leaving only the resistant strand. These leftovers were able to reproduce, passing on their gene of resistance. Over time, the population will consist of only methicillin-resistant S. aureus bacteria. This could not have happened if the random mutation had not spontaneously occurred, And there had not been natural selection (from the antibiotic) acting on the bacteria.
