Extinction

Extinction Extinction is defined as the permanent loss of all members of a species. A species is described as extinct when there are no remaining individuals of a species known to be left alive in the world... A [mass] extinction event is when at least half of all species are lost in a relatively short amount of geological time. An endemic species is one which is found in one geographical location and nowhere else.

Evidence Evidence from fossils, predominantly marine, indicate there have been 5 mass extinction events so far, The first of which as far back as 443 million years ago.

Physical Factors Most if not all on planet Earth have been cause by one or more physical or abiotic factor, Such as asteroids, which is a likely cause of the most well-known extinction events: the fall of the dinosaurs 66 million years ago. Other causes can be extreme changes in temperatures, that result in ice ages, followed by rapid global warming, Or volcanic activity, which might have cause 2/5 mass extinctions, 200 and 250 million years ago.

Abiotic Factors Other than physical factors, there are also biological factors, such as a new predator, which offsets the balance formed over millions of years, Causing the rate of deaths of a species to rise above the rate of births. Or new disease can cause such. A new disease, such as bird pox, can quickly spread through a species that has not developed immunity yet, wiping it out. An example of a disease like this affecting humans recently was was Covid 19, although we had the technology to developed a vaccine. There is also competition between species, for example in plants. Some plant life when introduced to a new environment can outgrow some existing plants, outcompeting them for space, sunlight, water, or nutrients, Killing off the other plant life. Habitat loss is another example. Destruction of habitats can leave animals without a home for safety, or food for survival. Just one of these factors is not always enough, but sometimes multiple factors, such as overhunting and habitat loss, can work in union to cause an extinction event for a species.

Human Influence One of the biggest causes modern day are humans. We cause the loss of biodiversity, directly via destruction of habitats, overhunting, or indirectly via global warming, climate change, and extreme weather conditions.
